Furniture prices in Pakistan: what decides the cost

The price comes from the material, the size, the finish and the hours of work. Two quotes only compare when all four match.

Written by the team at Muhammad Furnitures. Published 28 November 2026.

Short answer: The price comes from the material, the size, the finish and the hours of work. Two quotes only compare when all four match.

Why two prices for the same piece can differ

A quote for "a wardrobe" tells you little. One wardrobe may be 150 cm wide in board with a laminate finish. Another may be 210 cm wide with a solid wood frame and a polished finish. Both are wardrobes, and the prices will be far apart.

So before you compare prices, compare what the price includes.

What moves the price

  • Material. Solid wood usually costs more than plywood. Plywood usually costs more than MDF or particle board. Read plywood, MDF or solid wood for what each is good at.
  • Size. A wider, taller or deeper piece uses more material and takes more hours to build.
  • Finish. Natural polish, paint, veneer and laminate cost different amounts, and a finish with several coats takes longer.
  • Fittings. Hinges, drawer runners, sliding tracks and handles range from basic to heavy duty.
  • Fabric and foam. For sofas and bed headboards, the cover and the foam inside change the price as much as the frame does.
  • Detail. Extra drawers, shelves, mirrors, carving or lighting add work.

How to compare two quotes

  1. Ask for the exact size in centimetres, and check it is the same in both quotes.
  2. Ask what the frame, the back panel, the shelves and the drawer bottoms are made of.
  3. Ask what finish is used and how many coats.
  4. Ask what is included: delivery, fitting, chairs with a table, a mattress with a bed.
  5. Ask when the work will be ready and when each payment is due.

If one quote cannot answer these, it is not yet a price you can compare.

Ways to lower the cost without weakening the piece

  • Choose a simpler finish. A plain finish costs less than a multi-coat one.
  • Make the piece the size you need, not larger. Measure the room first with this guide.
  • Use solid wood where the piece carries weight, such as legs and frames, and board for panels that carry none.
  • Cut the number of drawers or shelves you will not use.

Common questions

Is solid wood always better than board?

No. Solid wood is strong and can be repaired, but it moves with the weather. Good board stays flat and suits large panels. Many pieces use both.

Why is a made-to-order price different from a shop price?

A shop price is for a fixed size and finish. A made-to-order price is for your size and your choices, so it can be lower or higher.
