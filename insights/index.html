Insights

Plain answers to the questions people ask before they order furniture.

Short, plain articles on the questions people ask before they order furniture: how to measure, which wood to pick, how big a sofa should be, and how to look after what you buy.

Each one answers a single question. For a wider overview, read the furniture buying guide.

How to measure a room before you order furniture

Measure the space where the piece will stand, then the doors and stairs it must pass through. Write every number in centimetres and take a photo.

Read the article

Sheesham, kikar and deodar: which wood for which piece

Sheesham is dense and polishes well. Kikar is hard and heavy. Deodar is lighter and softer. The right one depends on the piece, the room and your budget.

Plywood, MDF or solid wood: what to choose for each piece

Solid wood suits tables and bed frames. Plywood suits wardrobes and shelves. MDF suits painted surfaces, but keep it dry. Many pieces use more than one.

How big should your sofa set be?

Measure the wall the sofa will stand against, leave about 80 cm to walk, and keep about 40 cm between the sofa and the centre table.

Looking after wooden furniture in humid weather

Keep air moving, leave a small gap between furniture and wet walls, wipe spills at once, and clean with a dry or slightly damp cloth.

Made-to-order or ready-made furniture: which one should you buy?

Ready-made is faster and lets you see the piece first. Made-to-order fits your room and your choices better. Pick by how much your room needs a particular size.

How to plan the inside of a wardrobe

List what you store, measure your longest garment, and give each group its own space: hanging, folded, drawers and a shelf for bags and bedding.
